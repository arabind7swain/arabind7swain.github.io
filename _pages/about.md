I received my PhD from Emory University, under the supervison of [Ilya Nemenman](https://physics.emory.edu/people/bios/nemenman-ilya.html). My dissertaion was titled ["Detectability, Interpretability, and the Limits of Machine Learning in High-Dimensional Physical Systems"](https://etd.library.emory.edu/concern/etds/sn00b022v?locale=en), explores the fundamental boundaries of learning in complex multi modal data streams. My doctoral research focused on the Physics of AI/ML, utilizing tools from Random Matrix Theory and statistical mechanics to:

  * **Explain Inductive Biases:** Deconstruct how model architectures especially how embedding schemes interact with with data geometry.I studied their ability to predict the correct physical laws when models are applied to understand material properties. 

  * **Multi-Modal Efficiency:** Prove the mathematical advantages of multi-modal modeling over uni-modal approaches.

  * **Data Integration Schemas:** Analyze how different data fusion strategies for multiple data streams impact a model's data efficiency. 

Having recently relocated to India, I am currently seeking research or engineering roles where I can apply my background in high-performance model optimization and theoretical physics.
